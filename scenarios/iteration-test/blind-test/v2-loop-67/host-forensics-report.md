# Host/EDR Forensics Analyst — Authenticity Assessment
## Verdict
**Assessment:** Synthetic
**Verdict Confidence:** 93
**Synthetic-Confidence Score:** 90
## Executive Summary
The reviewed endpoint corpus is high-quality synthetic telemetry. Its Windows Security, Sysmon, eCAR, Linux syslog, and shell-history records are generally source-native and unusually strong in process-tree, session, identity, and lifecycle consistency. I found no hard causal contradiction: child activity does not precede its observed parent, process-dependent activity does not continue after termination, and successful SSH sessions have credible transport, authentication, shell, and close ordering.

The authenticity decision rests on two concrete fleet-wide fingerprints rather than narrative neatness or correlation completeness. First, all eight invalid-user SSH sessions preserve an identical three-digit microsecond suffix across four separately timestamped messages, even when the sequence spans 14–26 seconds. Ordinary successful SSH sessions do not show that behavior. This is characteristic of a single base timestamp advanced by whole-millisecond offsets. Second, unrelated Linux servers and workstations repeatedly expose the same IRQ-number-to-device catalog, including the exact mapping of IRQ 16 to `ahci`, IRQs 32/122 to NVMe queues, IRQs 45/154 to VirtIO input devices, IRQs 64/86/181 to Mellanox completion queues, and IRQs 24/137 to `ens160`/`ens192`. Seven hosts contain the complete ten-pair catalog and four more contain subsets. That cloned, internally eclectic hardware identity is not credible across this mixed fleet.

Those two independent patterns outweigh the corpus's otherwise excellent construction. The score therefore falls in the rubric's confidently synthetic range.
## Evidence For Synthetic
- **[distribution_texture] Invalid-user SSH timestamps are generated from a shared fractional-time seed.** The corpus contains eight invalid-user SSH PID groups with at least three records, and all eight have four records whose timestamps retain the same final three microsecond digits. Examples include:
  - `review-data/APP-INT-01.meridianhcs.local/syslog.log:107-110`: `13:40:39.995570`, `13:40:40.184570`, `13:40:40.817570`, and `13:40:40.957570` all end in `570`.
  - `review-data/DB-PROD-01.meridianhcs.local/syslog.log:211-214`: `17:38:22.163215` through `17:38:36.540215` all end in `215` despite a 14.377-second span.
  - `review-data/LT-MRIVERA-02.meridianhcs.local/syslog.log:53-56`: `13:20:43.680392` through `13:20:58.303392` all end in `392`.
  - `review-data/MAIL-EDGE-01.meridianhcs.local/syslog.log:309-312`: `16:47:58.298109` through `16:48:24.370109` all end in `109` despite a 26.072-second span.
  - The remaining four groups repeat the defect with suffixes `549` (FILE-LNX-01), `808` (LOG-MON-01), `998` (PROXY-01), and `742` (WEB-EXT-01).

  Across all SSH PID groups having at least three messages, the affected counts are APP 1/8, DB 1/6, FILE-LNX 1/5, LOG-MON 1/7, LT-MRIVERA 1/1, MAIL-EDGE 1/5, PROXY 1/9, and WEB-EXT 1/8; MAIL-CLIN has 0/3. The defect's confinement to every invalid-user path, rather than all syslog messages, is especially diagnostic of templated sequence timing.

- **[environment_or_collection_plausibility] The Linux fleet has a cloned IRQ/device identity.** `irqbalance` records expose the same exact ten mappings on APP-INT-01 (51 rows), DB-PROD-01 (71), FILE-LNX-01 (57), LOG-MON-01 (77), MAIL-CLIN-01 (36), PROXY-01 (45), and WEB-EXT-01 (118): `16/ahci`, `24/ens160`, `32/nvme0q1`, `45/virtio0-input`, `64/mlx5_comp0`, `86/mlx5_comp1`, `122/nvme0q2`, `137/ens192`, `154/virtio1-input`, and `181/mlx5_comp2`. MAIL-EDGE-01 has nine of the ten, while Linux workstation/laptop systems retain subsets of the same catalog. Concrete examples are APP's IRQ 122/NVMe at `12:04:28.214638`, IRQ 16/AHCI at `12:05:47.636518`, and IRQ 154/VirtIO at `12:15:00.886216`; DB repeats IRQ 137/ens192 at `12:05:23.517370` and IRQ 181/mlx5_comp2 at `12:18:18.348405`; WEB repeats IRQ 24/ens160 at `12:01:12.738162` and IRQ 45/virtio0-input at `12:10:04.847688`. Exact IRQ numbering is host-specific. Reusing this complete mixture of SATA, NVMe, VirtIO, Mellanox, and two identically numbered Ethernet interfaces across heterogeneous roles is a strong environment-generation fingerprint.

- **[weak_signal] Some Windows system-binary version metadata appears catalog-driven rather than host-driven.** WS-MCHEN-01 otherwise presents a Windows `10.0.22621.1` system-binary family, but its Sysmon Event ID 1 at `2024-03-18 13:59:22.137` reports `C:\Windows\System32\gpupdate.exe` as `10.0.19041.1`, and at `15:50:23.445` reports `C:\Windows\System32\gpresult.exe` as `10.0.20348.1`. WS-PPATEL-01, also predominantly `10.0.22621.1`, repeats the `gpupdate.exe` `10.0.19041.1` hash/version at `14:47:01.264`. Copied or preserved utilities can explain mixed versions, so this is not independently dispositive, but the repeated cross-build selection is consistent with a shared executable metadata catalog.
## Evidence For Real
- The corpus has substantial, varied endpoint volume rather than a thin attack-only trace: 33,946 eCAR records across 21 hosts, 18,529 Windows Security events across 10 Windows hosts, 11,690 Sysmon events across those hosts, 3,750 Linux syslog lines across 11 Linux hosts, and 700 shell-history lines. Per-host eCAR volume ranges from 262 on LT-MRIVERA-02 to 5,528 on DC-01; Linux syslog volume ranges from 156 to 1,193 lines.

- Windows XML is structurally source-native. All Security and Sysmon files parse as XML; provider names, channels, versions, task values, EventRecordIDs, and per-event field sets are coherent. Security includes plausible mixes of 4624/4625/4634 logons, 4648 explicit credentials, 4672 privilege assignment, 4688/4689 process lifecycle, 4720/4724/4726/4728/4738 account changes, 4768/4769 Kerberos activity, and 5140/5145 share access. Sysmon uses coherent schemas for Event IDs 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 13, and 22.

- Process identity and lifecycle are strong. Across all eCAR files, all 33,946 event IDs are unique. No examined child/dependent event precedes its referenced process creation, no dependent event occurs after that actor's termination, and no matched process object terminates before it is created. Sysmon likewise has no event using a ProcessGuid after its Event ID 5 termination. Boundary behavior is credible: `review-data/WS-AJOHNSON-01.meridianhcs.local/ecar.json:1` begins with termination of PID 5148, while the matching Sysmon Event ID 5 identifies `ssh.exe` and ProcessGuid `{fd907e59-219a-65f8-c602-0000ca1c89f9}` at `12:00:48.343`; the GUID's embedded epoch corresponds to a pre-window start, explaining the absence of a creation record in the capture.

- Windows process events exhibit plausible source delay rather than exact timestamp duplication. For example, all 128 DC-01 eCAR process creates have Sysmon Event ID 1 counterparts, with eCAR-minus-Sysmon offsets ranging from 11 to 806 ms and a 221.5 ms median. DC-02's 111 matched creates range from 4 to 576 ms with a 264 ms median. Similar near-complete matching with varied delays appears on FILE-SRV-01, MAIL-FIN-01, and the Windows workstations. A few endpoint-source misses remain, which is realistic collection texture.

- Identity reuse is coherent without collapsing user diversity. Domain users and machine accounts consistently use the SID prefix `S-1-5-21-1524654518-2022274387-1755902678`, with stable user RIDs across hosts. eCAR principal diversity ranges from four principals on several user workstations to 19 on DC-01 and 20 on DC-02, with service accounts, built-in principals, machine accounts, and named users appearing in role-appropriate proportions.

- Linux successful-session construction is operationally convincing. In `APP-INT-01/syslog.log:3-5`, SSH PID 2931577 records connection at `12:02:04.504467`, accepted password at `12:02:10.030893`, and PAM open at `12:02:10.112277`. eCAR records the inbound flow at `12:02:04.068`, USER_SESSION login for lina.nguyen at `12:02:11.000`, and child Bash PID 2931611 at `12:02:12.136`. Bash terminates at `12:56:15.942`, PAM closes at `13:01:15.967868`, the server process terminates at `13:01:21.775`, and eCAR logs logout at `13:01:28.170`. That ordering reflects distinct endpoint and syslog observations rather than a single copied timestamp.

- Bash history and process/file telemetry agree in source-appropriate ways. `DB-PROD-01/bash_history/root.bash_history` records a `mysqldump` redirection, gzip, and SCP sequence. eCAR omits shell redirection from the child executable command as expected, creates `/tmp/rpt_0318.sql` at `17:15:03.985`, creates `/tmp/rpt_0318.sql.gz` at `17:15:23.528`, opens SCP transport at `17:15:43.026`, reads the archive at `17:15:49.450`, and records target-side creation on APP-INT-01 at `17:15:55.530`.

- DC-01's audit-clear lifecycle is especially credible. Security Event ID 4688 shows the `net user svc_dirsync ... /add /domain` processes at `16:15:25`, followed by Event IDs 4720, 4724, 4738, and 4728 between `16:15:28.947` and `16:15:33.699`. eCAR later records `wevtutil cl Security` at `17:41:57.107/.252`; Security Event ID 1102 appears at `17:42:01.2672792` with EventRecordID 1, after the previous high record-ID sequence. Subsequent records restart at low IDs, and deletion processes at `17:50:04` precede Event ID 4726 at `17:50:08.737`. This is correct event-state and log-reset behavior.
## Detailed Analysis
**Process trees and execution causality.** Windows parentage follows credible boot, service, and interactive chains: `smss.exe`/`winlogon.exe`/`userinit.exe`/`explorer.exe` for users, `services.exe`/`svchost.exe` for service activity, and WMI- or shell-led descendants for administrative commands. Linux interactive commands descend from per-session Bash processes, while daemon work remains under service identities. I tested eCAR actor references, object identities, PIDs, images, creation times, and termination times across all hosts and found no orphan-after-termination or termination-before-creation defect.

**Session lifecycle.** Windows logon IDs, terminal session IDs, users, and process principals remain aligned through process activity. Linux SSH evidence distinguishes listener/privileged server processes, user shells, USER_SESSION objects, and transport tuples. Failed sessions remain pre-authentication and successful sessions open and close PAM state in a credible order. The failed-session timestamp microstructure is nevertheless artificial; its semantic order being correct does not neutralize that numeric defect.

**Security, Sysmon, and eCAR relationships.** Process-create telemetry generally agrees on PID, image, command line, parent, user, and logon identity while retaining millisecond-scale source delay. Security 4688 and Sysmon 1 records use appropriate source-specific fields rather than rendering as identical JSON. Sysmon ProcessGuids are host-distinct and temporally plausible. Security EventRecordIDs are monotonically increasing except for the explained DC-01 reset after Event ID 1102.

**User and system behavior.** The data contains human work, service traffic, scheduled jobs, remote administration, failed authentications, maintenance, file operations, browser/application activity, and varied process durations. Counts are not uniformly fixed: periodic sysstat jobs show occasional gaps and host-specific minute offsets, and workstation/server principal distributions differ. I did not treat attack reconstructability, record completeness, sanitized-looking domains, absent Sysmon types, or cross-source agreement as synthetic evidence.

**Linux source texture.** RFC 5424 framing, authpriv severities, SSH wording, PAM ordering, CRON activity, and Bash-history behavior are generally credible. The repeated IRQ/device catalog is the major exception: it leaks a shared environment template at the hardware-observation layer. The suffix-locked invalid-user timestamps leak a second template at the event-timing layer.

**Collection plausibility.** Sparse source-specific misses and timestamp latency are present, so the corpus does not contradict itself through implausibly perfect collection. The authenticity failure instead comes from repeated values that a collector would preserve from different machines and independent log calls, not normalize into exact fleet-wide IRQ assignments or failure-path-only fractional-time suffixes.

## Realism Score by Category

- **Temporal patterns:** 5 — lifecycle order and source delay are strong, but the invalid-user SSH microsecond-suffix lock is a highly diagnostic generated-time artifact.

## Recommendations
- Build Linux hardware observations from per-host inventories. Enforce uniqueness or justified template families for IRQ numbers, storage controllers, virtual devices, NIC names, NUMA topology, and accelerator queues; do not combine every device family into one universal catalog.
- Timestamp each syslog occurrence independently at the source's native precision. Add a detector that flags three or more messages in a PID/session sequence when all preserve the same sub-millisecond suffix despite nonzero millisecond or multi-second offsets.
- Keep Windows executable version and hash metadata tied to a host/build-specific file inventory. Review `gpupdate.exe`, `gpresult.exe`, and `mstsc.exe` selections on 22621-family systems.
- Preserve the existing strengths: action-to-process causality, ProcessGuid lifecycle, audit-log reset semantics, SID continuity, shell redirection handling, source-specific timing latency, and realistic partial collection.
- Add corpus-level authenticity tests for fleet-wide hardware-map duplication and event-family-specific timestamp fingerprints; per-record schema validation will not catch either defect.
