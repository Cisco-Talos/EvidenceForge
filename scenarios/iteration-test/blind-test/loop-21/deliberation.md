## Key Agreements

- **All four revised verdicts are Synthetic, with meaningful uncertainty.** Scores range from 68 to 75, within the experts' likely-synthetic band. None establishes a hard contradiction, impossible OS sequence, or generator identity leak.
- **The strongest common endpoint concern is exact native fork-time texture.** All four confirmed the three groups across two Macs: ten of 62 visible zsh-originated forks retain identical microsecond fractions within each group. The first group preserves `.198588000` across 27-, 35- and 19-second gaps; corresponding Mach differences are exactly 648,000,000, 840,000,000 and 456,000,000 ticks at the apparent 24 MHz scale. These are distinct child PID/pidversion notifications, not duplicate observations of one fork. Scripted scheduling and timestamp reconstruction remain possible explanations; a transformation affecting both clocks is not excluded.
- **Source-local integrity and broader authenticity concerns can coexist.** Successful process creation/dependency/termination checks, useful native and normalized identity correlations, and protocol/file records fitting their own connections do not test statistical timestamp texture or conservation across two proxy connections. The successful checks remain substantive strengths.
- **Concrete proxy examples warrant qualified scrutiny.** All four checked examples in which the client totals equal CONNECT control plus tunnel counters but the inferred upstream differs. Pairing uses timing/target/SNI, not an explicit common cross-leg identity. Inspection, counter scope, buffering/discarding and pairing error limit the conclusion. The Network analyst's corrected scope is detailed below.
- **Windows deployment patterns are supporting evidence, not impossible configurations.** All four confirmed twelve System32 MpCmdRun.exe launches across ten hosts, with services.exe parentage and missing version resources, plus four netsvcs/SYSTEM CryptSvc launches. Copied binaries, wrappers or custom service configuration remain possible; installed-file and service-configuration evidence is insufficient to rule them out.
- **The upload is a lifecycle tension, not proof of a dead process operating.** Curl exits at 16:35:34.950; the gzip observation starts at 16:35:34.699640 and lasts 10.069288 seconds, with response observation at 16:35:48.933566. All preserve send-buffering and canceled-client/proxy-continuation explanations. HTTP 200 does not prove that curl consumed the response, and the later endpoint FLOW lacks process identity.
- **Small anomalies retain small or qualified weight.** The CUPS numeric slot, 4658 ProcessName addition and isolated SMB mapping orphan were corroborated, with diagnostic/enrichment/export/window explanations preserved. Premature DNS refreshes and the single 9.227-second chmod do not materially strengthen most revised assessments. No expert uses narrative clarity, rich correlation, domain sanitization, sparse optional sources or slice-boundary omissions as synthetic evidence.

## Key Disagreements

The initial verdict split resolves in the actual revised positions, but weighting and measurement scope remain unsettled.

**Proxy prevalence requires a material correction.** The Network analyst reproduced the original 1,208 pairs and 698 discrepancies, then found that the population pooled 619 `tunnel` pairs with 589 `tunnel-setup` pairs. All 589 setup pairs differed, but those rows explicitly include `ssl_bump=peek` and a `tunnel_id`; the collection also contains 2,123 `ssl-inspect` rows. The analyst therefore withdraws the characterization of 698/1,208 as plain transparent-tunnel failures. Positive evidence of an inspection-aware path makes the alternative explanation more consequential than the original report allowed.

The Network analyst retains **109 discrepancies among 619 plain-tunnel pairs (17.6%)**, including **65 among 332** with both histories exactly ShADadfF. Separately, the Detection Engineer's stricter matching found **93/539 (17.3%) across 22 clients**, including two examples exceeding the entire downstream originator input. The Threat Hunter reports the same stricter 93/539 result and a less restrictive 109/627 result. Different eligibility and access-row matching rules produce different populations; these rates are not interchangeable. Host/EDR checked the named examples and explicitly did not rerun the full aggregate. The other responses do not react to the Network analyst's later correction, so no panel endorsement of the corrected denominator is claimed.

**Relative weight differs.** Host/EDR retains the highest final score, 75, emphasizing timing, Windows representations and checked proxy examples. The other scores are 68–70, also emphasizing timing and proxy concerns while preserving substantial collection/production alternatives. Host/EDR adds no meaningful weight from the lone chmod; the Threat Hunter retains it only as weak evidence. There is no shared quantitative allocation of score to each finding.

**The SMB reference statements concern different populations.** Detection's single orphan mapping UID does not contradict successful smb_files joins. Network explicitly narrows its earlier completeness statement: its initial check covered smb_files, not every mapping row. All treat the orphan's authenticity weight as low.

## Most Convincing Evidence

The ordering below summarizes the experts' stated weighting and corroboration; it is not a recorded panel vote.

1. **Exact native macOS fork phases — distribution texture.** Independently confirmed by all four, this caused the largest change for the two initially inconclusive reviewers and supplied the Network analyst's strongest new evidence. Distinct fork notifications retain exact microsecond phase and corresponding integer-second Mach intervals across three groups on two hosts. A coherent timestamp transformation or controlled scheduling remains possible; this is not an impossibility finding.
2. **Residual plain-tunnel stream-accounting discrepancies — contract gap.** The most restrictive shared example is 13:17:01: client UID CG0yFMvovjlI1Trm11 has 786 originator bytes, comprising 422 control plus 364 tunnel bytes, while inferred upstream C94XuVK3KPtstF6zdLv has 816. Both are successful, zero-missed-byte flows with ordinary histories. Unforwarded server tail data cannot alone explain extra originator input in a transparent relay. Inferred pairing and inspection/counter semantics remain limitations; use the corrected plain-tunnel population, not 698/1,208 as a transparent-tunnel rate.
3. **Repeated atypical Windows deployment representations — environmental plausibility.** Twelve MpCmdRun launches and four CryptSvc launches repeat the same unusual placement or service configuration. All verified the populations, while retaining copied-binary and custom-service alternatives. These support rather than determine the verdict.
4. **Curl termination before most upload/response observation — contract gap.** All corroborated the specific transaction and early endpoint termination. Ordinary synchronous-client behavior is strained, but no exit status, proven response consumption or exact socket ownership eliminates buffered transmission and proxy continuation.
5. **Strong process and source-local network consistency — counterevidence.** Visible process identities and lifecycle ordering, platform-specific fork/exec semantics, useful session pivots and directional per-flow file/body bounds remain intact in the checks performed. These strengths restrain every revised score and explain why none claims near-certain synthetic origin.

## Most Debated Points

- **Scheduling versus native timestamp construction:** Real scripts can schedule commands, but exact microsecond equality across distinct forks and multiple bursts is more specific than ordinary integer-second scheduling. Matching Mach time defeats simple display-rounding explanations, yet cannot independently exclude a transformation that reconstructs both clocks. Windows phase retention is related timing evidence and is not counted repeatedly as a separate independent defect.
- **Opaque forwarding versus inspection-aware proxy behavior:** Plain CONNECT relays should conserve the tunneled directional stream; two inspected TLS sessions need not. The Network analyst's action classification correction removes 589 setup rows from the plain-tunnel argument. The surviving named examples and narrower populations remain concerning under their tunnel labeling, with inferred pairing and instrumentation alternatives unresolved.
- **Successful transport versus successful client completion:** SF closure and HTTP 200 describe observed network outcomes, not necessarily successful response consumption by the terminated curl process. The experts consistently reject promoting the upload into a proven post-termination operation.
- **Native source shape versus collector enrichment:** All 44 4658 rows contain ProcessName inside EventData, but enrichment is a viable explanation. Likewise, eleven CUPS messages have six-digit numeric slots without enough message/collector context to establish impossible values. These remain secondary fidelity questions.
- **Policy wording and collection boundaries:** Host/EDR explicitly withdraws the overdefinite “rejected application” wording and retains no denial/child-execution contradiction. Sparse selected sources, pre-window origins, outside-window conn logging and optional audit families cannot be treated as unexplained absence by default.

## Improvement Recommendations (Consensus)

These recommendations reflect overlap in the actual responses, conditional on the data being synthetic; they were not jointly voted on.

1. **Derive native fork timestamps from an execution timeline with realistic scheduling overhead.** Keep wall and Mach clocks coherent. Preserve genuine scripted cadence, while representing actual subsecond execution effects rather than retaining exact planned fractions or perturbing only one clock. All four prioritize this.
2. **Use one directional byte ledger for transparent CONNECT legs and access counters, with explicit proxy semantics.** Separate CONNECT control bytes from tunnel bytes. Validate plain tunnel, setup/peek and inspection sessions separately; represent interception, discarded/unforwarded data and counter scope explicitly. All four recommend coherent cross-leg accounting; the Network analyst's correction specifically requires excluding inspection setup from plain-relay equality checks.
3. **Tie upload-client termination to its actual outcome.** For completion, retain the client through its request/response lifecycle; for cancellation or failure, represent the outcome and any buffered transmission or proxy continuation. All four recommend this distinction without treating a server-side 200 as proof of client consumption.
4. **Model Windows executable deployment and service identity together.** Use the installed Defender tool location/launch mechanism and host-appropriate CryptSvc group/account; make copied tools or custom services explicit when intended. All four identify these as supporting concerns; Threat Hunter, Detection and Host/EDR explicitly recommend deployment-specific correction.
5. **Preserve the existing correlation and lifecycle strengths.** Retain coherent process GUID/PID/image and session identities, native fork/exec distinctions, per-source timing, denial semantics and useful RDP/SSH/database/SMB continuity. Do not introduce arbitrary missing companions, forced byte equality for intercepted sessions or artificial disorder to conceal regularity.

Lower-priority items are not promoted to consensus priorities: Detection and Host/EDR explicitly recommend keeping XML enrichment separate from native EventData; Detection additionally recommends explaining or correcting the CUPS numeric slot and the isolated SMB mapping orphan. The other experts corroborate these observations but give them limited weight. The responses do not support broad suppression of pre-TTL DNS queries or a fleet-wide runtime correction based on one slow chmod.
