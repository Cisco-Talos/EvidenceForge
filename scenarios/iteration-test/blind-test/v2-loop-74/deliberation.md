**Round 1 — initial positions and strongest evidence.**

- **Threat Hunter:** Initially Inconclusive (verdict confidence 82; synthetic-confidence 47). The strongest production-like evidence was coherent attack ordering across endpoint, authentication, network, firewall, proxy, and application records; zero tested eCAR actor-lifetime violations; and clean Zeek protocol-child and ASA lifecycle relationships. The strongest synthetic evidence was environmental and distributional: `irqbalance` supplied 561 of 3,756 Linux syslog records, MicroK8s/snapd appeared on ten heterogeneous Linux hosts, and `LOG-MON-01` made 168 unauthenticated Wget proxy requests to a broad destination pool. Unique observations were smooth role-scaled Windows Type 5 logon volume and an estate-wide half-hour sysstat lattice.
- **Detection Engineer:** Initially Synthetic (verdict confidence 86; synthetic-confidence 74). The strongest evidence was two physically implausible 0.218 ms and 1.1531 ms Windows lock/unlock cycles, each lacking the same-cycle Type 7 logon seen before normal unlocks, plus durable `systemd-resolved` TCP-fallback messages followed by repeated successful same-PID UDP DNS to the same server. The unique fleet-level observation was exactly four resolver-state messages on every resolver-logging host, reinforced by probabilistic-looking omissions in otherwise fixed sysstat schedules.
- **Network Forensics:** Initially Synthetic (verdict confidence 90; synthetic-confidence 84). The decisive evidence was repeated disagreement between inspected-proxy tunnel counters and Zeek payload totals on the same tuple, including 118 gross cases, followed by repeated approximately 600 ms connection and intra-UID HTTP cadence. Unique observations included five CONNECT IDS alerts without corresponding Zeek HTTP analyzer evidence, 39 established non-resumed TLS 1.2 rows with certificate history but no chain FUID, and seven directly referenced application FUIDs absent from `files.json`. The analyst nevertheless found strong Zeek state, byte, DNS, PKI, sensor-skew, and ASA semantics.
- **Host/EDR Forensics:** Initially Synthetic (verdict confidence 82; synthetic-confidence 66). The analyst independently found the same two impossible lock/unlock cycles, six `crashpad_handler.exe` records incorrectly represented as module loads on four workstations, and seven in-window SSH sessions across six hosts with only close-side syslog despite eCAR-confirmed transport, process creation, and login. The unique process-ownership finding was an OpenSSH privileged monitor PID changing identity and directly owning a transferred file; the unique timing observation was universal Sysmon-before-Security ordering across all 987 matched process creates.

**Round 3 — revised positions.**

- **Threat Hunter:** Revised from Inconclusive to Synthetic. The two independently corroborated Windows unlock failures, the repeated resolver-state contradiction, the executable-as-module error, the SSH lifecycle omissions, and especially the verified proxy/Zeek accounting mismatch supplied the concrete local defects absent from the hunter's broad lifecycle checks. Verdict confidence rose to 86 and synthetic-confidence to 69; the score remains the panel's lowest because the attack lifecycle, source-native formats, and most cross-source contracts remain unusually strong.
- **Detection Engineer:** Retained Synthetic. Independent host confirmation of both unlock failures and the additional proxy-accounting and endpoint-semantic defects reinforced the original assessment. Verdict confidence rose to 91 and synthetic-confidence to 80.
- **Network Forensics:** Retained Synthetic, with verdict confidence rising to 92 because the host and detection findings show independent defect families outside the network sources. Synthetic-confidence decreased slightly to 82 because a direct frozen-data check showed that all 39 cited TLS 1.2 missing-chain cases had nonzero `missed_bytes` (14–2,810 bytes), making capture loss a materially stronger alternative explanation for that particular finding.
- **Host/EDR Forensics:** Retained Synthetic. The verified proxy accounting mismatch and repeated resolver-state inconsistency broadened the case beyond endpoint defects, raising verdict confidence to 88 and synthetic-confidence to 74. Strong process trees, hash stability, and ordinary session lifecycles continue to constrain the score.

The final verdict converged on Synthetic, but the panel did not converge on equal evidentiary strength: final synthetic-confidence spans 69–82. The convergence reflects accumulation of independent, concrete defect families rather than narrative neatness, correlation completeness, or missing pre-window initiators.

## Key Agreements

- The two sub-1.2-ms Windows 4800-to-4801 cycles are not plausible human lock/unlock behavior. The absence of a same-cycle Type 7 4624 is probative because four normal unlocks in the same collection include that event 0.213–0.511 seconds before 4801.
- The collection is highly accomplished at source-native structure. Windows/Sysmon schemas, process GUIDs and hashes, Zeek state/history and packet accounting, ASA build/teardown arithmetic, DNS response logic, and timestamp precision are largely credible.
- Most major operational lifecycles are coherent and huntable. The reverse shell, SSH pivot and scan, PsExec/WMI activity, account lifecycle, Security-log clear, staging, and proxy upload generally preserve plausible actor, process, session, tuple, and temporal relationships.
- The synthetic case does not rest on complete correlation or an easy-to-follow attack narrative. It rests on visible timing impossibilities, incompatible state/accounting relationships, source-native semantic errors, lifecycle-shaped omissions, and repeated distribution texture.
- Repetition across hosts materially increases the weight of otherwise explainable anomalies: resolver quotas and fallback behavior, cloned Linux service noise, fixed scheduling texture, and approximately 600 ms client timing are more suspicious as families than as isolated records.

## Key Disagreements

- **Broad lifecycle integrity versus local contradictions:** The Threat Hunter found zero violations in tested eCAR actor lifetimes, Sysmon process dependencies, Zeek child ordering, and ASA durations. Detection and Host/EDR identified a different contract—the Windows lock/Type 7/unlock lifecycle—with two concrete failures. These findings are compatible: broad graph integrity remains strong, but it does not negate a source-local lifecycle defect the broad checks did not test.
- **Proxy counters versus Zeek payload:** The Network analyst treated the discrepancy as decisive. A direct check confirmed that tunnel `PT-b8882fb4c111f8ca` has non-CONNECT proxy rows totaling 644 client and 156,588 server bytes, exactly matching its tunnel counters, while the same client/proxy tuple in Zeek UID `CqxDm2DrpeoRDlICa2` records 4,453 and 793,756 payload bytes with `missed_bytes=0`. Different proxy and transport byte scopes, transformation, compression, or unlogged transactions are possible alternatives, so the panel stops short of calling every mismatch physically impossible; the magnitude, repetition, zero-loss examples, and exact proxy-side reconciliation nevertheless make it a high-weight contradiction unless the source contract explicitly explains the scopes.
- **TLS certificate-chain omissions:** The Network analyst viewed 39 TLS 1.2 rows with an `X` history marker but no chain as a moderate contract gap. The Threat Hunter noted packet loss as a collection explanation. Direct review-data checking found nonzero `missed_bytes` for all 39 cases, so the panel downgrades this from a material synthetic indicator to an ambiguous collection/parser issue. The seven absent directly referenced application FUIDs remain a separate, narrower concern, especially the cited zero-loss 137 MB MSI transfer.
- **Resolver fallback semantics:** The Detection Engineer treated continued same-PID UDP to the same DNS server after a TCP-fallback message as a repeated contract failure. A silent resolver recovery or missing state-transition log could explain one occurrence, but 63 later UDP flows on one host and recurrence on two others, combined with exactly four resolver-state messages per host, make the omission pattern difficult to dismiss as ordinary loss. The panel regards this as strong but not physically impossible evidence.
- **Environmental texture:** MicroK8s across unrelated roles, dominant `irqbalance` chatter, LOG-MON Wget traffic, role-scaled Type 5 logons, and fixed sysstat cadence may reflect standardized images, centralized schedules, package mirrors, or synthetic monitoring. The Threat Hunter weighted these enough to remain initially Inconclusive, while other specialists treated them mainly as reinforcement. The panel agrees they are medium-to-low weight unless paired with a visible state or role contradiction.
- **Universal provider ordering:** All 987 matched process creations place Sysmon before Security by 35–899 ms. Host/EDR considered this modest generated texture; the other reports emphasized that stable provider/collector latency can naturally impose one-way ordering. It remains a weak signal only.

## Most Convincing Evidence

1. **Windows lock/unlock contradiction:** Two independent experts identified the same two 0.218 ms and 1.1531 ms lock/unlock cycles, with no same-cycle Type 7 logon despite normal examples demonstrating that the audit stream records it. This is the clearest physically implausible and source-locally testable defect.
2. **Inspected-proxy/Zeek accounting mismatch:** Hundreds of same-tuple disagreements include zero-loss examples differing by hundreds of kilobytes, while the proxy's transaction rows exactly reconcile to its own tunnel totals. The cited 15:33:46 example was directly verified. Its weight is slightly limited by the possibility of undocumented counter-scope semantics.
3. **Resolver state versus observed transport:** Three hosts continue successful same-PID UDP DNS to the named server after declaring TCP fallback, without a visible recovery transition; one host has 63 later flows. Independent endpoint and Zeek records make an unrelated application explanation untenable.
4. **Repeated endpoint semantic and lifecycle defects:** `crashpad_handler.exe` is represented six times as a loaded module rather than a helper process, and seven eCAR-proven in-window SSH sessions retain close-side syslog while losing connection/auth/open evidence. These are repeated source-native or lifecycle-shaped defects across multiple hosts.
5. **Production-like contracts that limit certainty:** Clean process ancestry, hash stability, Zeek tuple and packet semantics, sensor-specific skew/loss, ASA lifecycles, DNS behavior, and detailed attack pivots are substantial evidence for realism. They prevent the panel from assigning near-certain synthetic-confidence despite the defects above.

## Most Debated Points

- Whether proxy tunnel counters and Zeek payload bytes are intended to measure the same layer. Without an explicit source contract, the mismatch is highly suspicious but admits a semantics-based alternative explanation.
- Whether a resolver can silently resume UDP after a logged TCP fallback. A single missing transition is plausible; repeated same-PID behavior plus identical per-host state-message quotas makes the family much harder to explain.
- Whether TLS certificate history without a chain is anomalous when packets were missed. The frozen-data check materially favored packet loss as the alternative explanation, so this point no longer drives the verdict.
- Whether estate-wide MicroK8s, irqbalance, sysstat, and Wget patterns reflect a standardized operational environment or reusable generation pools. No single pattern is impossible, but their combined cross-role regularity remains conspicuous.
- How much weight to assign the collection's unusually complete and correct correlations. The panel agrees completeness alone is not synthetic evidence; correctness is affirmative realism evidence, but it cannot erase specific contradictions elsewhere.

## Improvement Recommendations (Consensus)

1. Build one explicit accounting contract for each proxied connection. Define every proxy byte field's layer and scope, derive proxy and Zeek views from shared connection truth, model TLS/control overhead and transformations explicitly, and add same-tuple invariants for inspected tunnels, including large zero-loss transfers.
2. Generate Windows lock, Type 7 unlock logon, and Event 4801 from one guarded session lifecycle. Enforce a human-scale locked interval, require the same LogonID and session, and reject sub-second lock/unlock pairs unless an explicitly modeled non-human source-native condition justifies them.
3. Make resolver feature state durable per host and DNS server. After a TCP-fallback message, keep that resolver's traffic on TCP until a modeled recovery/probe transition occurs, and derive state messages from actual DNS outcomes rather than fixed per-host quotas.
4. Correct endpoint ownership and source-local lifecycle semantics. Represent Crashpad as a child executable with its own process identity; keep OpenSSH privileged monitor and user-context transfer processes distinct; and make SSH syslog observation atomic enough that retained closes for in-window sessions do not repeatedly lose all open-side evidence without a visible collection gap.
5. Remove quantized and cloned background texture at the family level. Replace approximately 600 ms client steps and fixed resolver quotas with stateful, per-client scheduling; vary Linux daemon/service mixtures by host role; reduce dominant small message pools; and tie MicroK8s, monitoring Wget destinations, Type 5 churn, and scheduled-task omissions to observable workloads or outages.
6. Preserve the strongest realism contracts as regressions: process ancestry and lifetimes, per-host hash stability, source-native Windows schemas, Zeek UID/tuple/state/packet accounting, sensor skew and packet-loss markers, ASA connection lifecycles, DNS/PKI validity, and the existing attack-chain ordering.
