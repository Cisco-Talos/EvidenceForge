## Key Agreements

- **The repeated native fork phases are the strongest authenticity concern.** All four revised responses verify the same ten distinct child creations: MAC-MROSS-01 ES lines 35/38/41/44 retain `.198588000`, lines 53/56/59 retain `.487145000`, and MAC-MROSS-02 lines 83/86/89 retain `.965208000`. The groups account for seven of 26 and three of 35 zsh-parent forks respectively. Their inter-fork gaps are 19–41 seconds, while subsequent exec notifications have varying subsecond offsets. Agreement corroborates one observation; it does not create four independent defects.
- **The finding is strong distribution texture, not a hard contradiction.** All four retain deliberate absolute-deadline automation, controlled replay or group-aware timestamp reconstruction/export as viable alternatives. The supplied evidence does not establish such a transformation, and simple whole-stream timestamp rounding does not explain the variable exec offsets.
- **Most checked technical relationships remain realistic.** The revised responses retain substantial credit for Windows source shapes, process identities and visible lifetimes; macOS fork/exec/exit semantics; SSH ordering; protocol joins; payload budgets; differentiated sensor roles; and certificate relationships. Coherence is neither suspicious by itself nor sufficient to establish production origin.
- **The seven cat-based PDF/Office reads are one secondary behavioral observation.** All final responses give this limited support, with legitimate raw-byte inspection or automation as alternatives. No expert establishes impossible file access or requires missing GUI telemetry. Filename extensions alone do not establish contents.
- **No reviewed report establishes an impossible execution, broad causal collapse, generator identity leak or source-native schema contradiction.** The missing SMB connection companion receives no synthetic penalty without a relevant collection guarantee. Optional coverage, complete attack narratives and ordinary scheduled sysstat cadence are not authenticity defects.

## Key Disagreements

The initial classification split was three Inconclusive judgments versus the Host/EDR analyst's Synthetic judgment. The original inconclusive reports had not specifically assessed the group-specific fork phases. Once each rechecked that evidence, all three revised their classification. This resolves the submitted verdict disagreement without requiring the facilitator to infer any additional votes.

The remaining difference is degree and evidentiary weight: final synthetic-confidence scores range from 63 to 67, and verdict confidence ranges from 74 to 82. Host/EDR retains the highest score, while the other specialists move only moderately into the likely-synthetic band. All preserve viable automation/export explanations and strong technical realism; none claims certain synthetic origin.

The reports also differ in secondary emphasis. Network Forensics retains TCP residual texture as weak supporting evidence, and other experts do not elevate it into an independently strong defect. Threat Hunter originally assigned small weight to the malformed SQL and all-zero exits; the other revised responses assign no material additional penalty, and the Threat Hunter does not upgrade them. Detection explicitly withdraws general clock conversion as an independent scoring reason. These are qualifications and weighting differences, not contradictory record-level findings.

## Most Convincing Evidence

The following ranking reflects the emphasis in the actual revised responses, rather than an additional numerical ranking vote:

1. **Exact group-specific phases in ten native fork occurrence times — strongest evidence favoring synthetic construction or reconstructed timing.** Three groups on two hosts preserve arbitrary microsecond fractions over 19–41-second gaps, with varied following exec delays. This was independently rechecked by all four and drove all three changed verdicts. Automation/replay remains the principal alternative.
2. **Coherent source-native identities, lifetimes and network budgets — strongest evidence of technical realism and the main limit on origin confidence.** The 921 Windows process-create pairs, visible endpoint lifetime checks, SSH ordering, protocol tuple/interval joins and transport/body scopes survive review. Network certificate and TLS checks add specialty-specific support. These findings remain credited after the verdict changes; they demonstrate fidelity, not provenance.
3. **Seven document-class reads represented by cat on one Mac — modest evidence favoring generic activity modeling.** Repeated access through one utility is narrow, but technically valid raw inspection or selected automation can explain it. Multiple reports concern the same seven operations, counted once.
4. **TCP accounting residual clustering — weak supporting texture.** Network Forensics finds repeated small residual combinations across sensors and within one MySQL pair, while also finding substantial variation and no impossible budget. Common options, segmentation and capture behavior prevent stronger attribution. Other experts retain that limited interpretation.

## Most Debated Points

### Round 2 — Cross-examination and strength challenges

- **Does correct causal ordering contradict artificial timing?** The initial evidence can appear to point in opposite directions, but the revised responses agree that these test different properties. A dataset can have sound lifecycles, identities and network accounting while command initiations retain constructed timing. The fork phases are the stronger evidence for the changed origin inference; lifecycle checks continue to limit severity. No checked relationship is reclassified as faulty simply because the verdict changes.
- **Can an ordinary clock conversion explain the fork phases?** A fixed 24 MHz conversion can explain exact agreement between wall and mach representations and therefore is not an independent defect. It does not explain why distinct forks retain a group-specific arbitrary fractional phase while exec offsets vary. All four distinguish these claims; Host/EDR counts the wall/mach confirmation once, and Detection withdraws conversion as separate score support.
- **Could real scheduling or replay explain the strongest signal?** All four accept that possibility. An absolute-deadline harness or group-aware transformation could produce the pattern from real executions. The exact repeated phases across three groups are more specific than ordinary periodicity or shell sleeps, but the panel does not label them impossible or calculate a nominal probability under unsupported independence assumptions. A documented transformation could materially weaken the inference about origin.
- **Is cat on PDF/Office files inherently implausible?** No. The narrower concern is repeated operation-mechanism choice, not technical capability. Host/EDR lowers its score partly because the other reports sharpen this alternative explanation. The experts reject fabricated GUI companions or arbitrary diversity as remedies.
- **Do packet residuals, invalid SQL, zero exits or one missing companion become decisive together?** No expert upgrades these to hard contradictions. TCP headers and capture effects explain plausible accounting variation; an invalid query can be an operator error without successful output; selected workloads can exit normally; one absent connection row can reflect filtering or collection loss. Repetition across reports does not multiply their weight.

## Improvement Recommendations (Consensus)

The recommendations below recur across all four revised responses. They are conditional on the intended data origin and preserve the experts' qualifications.

1. **Derive each native fork occurrence from the command scheduling and execution timeline.** Avoid inheriting one arbitrary microsecond fraction through integer-second offsets for an entire command group. Represent runtime and scheduler effects at the timing owner, while preserving fork–exec–exit ordering, parent/child identity and already-correct cross-source relationships. Do not independently jitter wall and mach clocks or add random noise merely for appearance.
2. **Document replay or timestamp reconstruction when it is intentional.** Specify any group scheduling, absolute-deadline harness or export transformation that affects native occurrence times, so these timestamps are interpreted under the correct provenance and precision policy. This directly addresses the strongest competing explanation rather than assuming the underlying activity was synthetic.
3. **Match the seven Mac document accesses to the intended operation and tool.** Retain cat when raw byte-stream reading or collection is intended; use the actual reader, application, file-provider or other mechanism when a different operation is intended. Do not create GUI evidence solely to diversify observed commands or enlarge collection coverage.
4. **Preserve the technical fidelity already established.** Retain source-native Windows shapes, macOS process semantics, stable identities, working SSH/session lifecycles, tuple and protocol intervals, certificate reuse and correct byte scopes. Do not add arbitrary failures, remove matching evidence or alter narrative completeness to imitate messiness.

The following remain narrower, conditional follow-ups rather than panel-wide proven defects: Network Forensics recommends checking residual accounting against representative packet traces and clarifying the single unresolved SMB mapping companion; Threat Hunter recommends valid SQL only if the command is intended to succeed and outcome-derived exit statuses if modeling a broader workload. Their respective alternatives and limited evidentiary weight remain intact.
