**Round 1 — initial positions**

- **Threat Hunter:** Inconclusive (verdict confidence 78; synthetic-confidence 44). The strongest authenticity evidence was coherent attack and process/session ordering, credible two-leg proxy exfiltration and byte accounting, independent sensor clock offsets and UIDs, and a realistic Security-log clear/reset sequence. The strongest adverse evidence was fleet-wide Linux `debian-sa1` regularity, smooth cross-role PID growth, millisecond-separated endpoint repeats, and a Type 9 outbound identity of `marcus.chen` that did not carry into the proxy attribution of the upload. Unique emphasis: operational pivotability, lifecycle completeness, and role-sensitive background contention.
- **Detection Engineer:** Synthetic (95; 97). The decisive evidence was software that the expert placed in release trains later than the 18 March 2024 event date, a new-Teams version combined with the classic `Teams.exe` path and executable, and mixed Windows core-binary build families on nominally 22621 workstations. Anonymous proxy groups alternating among 407, 403, and 200 without a visible authenticated state added supporting weight. Unique emphasis: software chronology, product-generation consistency, event/schema semantics, and Postfix queue-chain fidelity.
- **Network Forensics:** Synthetic (94; 89). The strongest finding was that 416 of 639 uniquely keyed, completed client-to-proxy CONNECT sessions reported a tunnel duration longer than the carrying Zeek TCP session; 262 of those also had exact two-way byte equality. Near-exact 600 ms pacing appeared in 131 adjacent connection starts, forming 30 runs across eight clients, with the same cadence in persistent-session transactions. Five dangling HTTP FUID references and a very stable inter-sensor offset were lower-weight concerns. Unique emphasis: transport/application lifecycle bounds, interarrival texture, and source-local protocol contracts.
- **Host/EDR Forensics:** Synthetic (84; 78). The strongest evidence was a universal one-to-three-frame ceiling across 713 Sysmon Event 10 call traces, selective missing hashes in 19 of 972 process starts, all-five-field PE metadata gaps in 100 starts, and arithmetic timing in ProcessAccess and failed-logon bursts. The expert also noted bounded SSH authentication delays and an awkward database archive sequence. Unique emphasis: endpoint source-native texture, acquisition/enrichment behavior, and process/session causality.

**Round 3 — revised positions**

- **Threat Hunter:** Changes from Inconclusive/44 to Synthetic/90, with verdict confidence rising to 91. The verified proxy contradiction directly overturns the initial statement that no hard contradiction was present: a CONNECT tunnel cannot outlive the completed TCP connection carrying it, and exact byte agreement makes mismatching an implausible explanation. The independently confirmed endpoint-wide call-stack and metadata regularities add breadth beyond the hunter's original Linux texture findings.
- **Detection Engineer:** Remains Synthetic, moving from 97 to 98 synthetic-confidence and from 95 to 97 verdict confidence. The software-version and host-image findings remain decisive in this expert's specialty, while the independently reproduced proxy lifecycle contradiction supplies a separate, non-endpoint reason for the same verdict. The exact software release-date mapping cannot be re-proven from log contents alone, but the corpus does confirm the cited dates, versions, Teams path, and repeated host distribution.
- **Network Forensics:** Remains Synthetic, moving from 89 to 94 synthetic-confidence and from 94 to 96 verdict confidence. Reproduction of all reported proxy-duration and 600 ms pacing counts reinforces the original position; the confirmed 713-record call-stack ceiling and concentrated endpoint enrichment gaps show that the problem is not confined to one network family.
- **Host/EDR Forensics:** Remains Synthetic, moving from 78 to 90 synthetic-confidence and from 84 to 91 verdict confidence. The host findings alone supported “likely synthetic”; the systemic, independently verified transport impossibility moves the whole dataset into the confidently synthetic band. Detection's product-generation evidence also strengthens the host-image concern, although its external release chronology remains specialist testimony rather than a fact independently derivable from the permitted corpus.

## Key Agreements

**Round 4 — consensus summary**

- After cross-examination, all four experts assess the dataset as synthetic, and all final synthetic-confidence scores fall in the 81–100 “confidently synthetic” band. This is a genuine revision by the Threat Hunter, not forced averaging.
- The corpus has substantial production-like strengths: source-native schemas, coherent process/session lifecycles, realistic firewall state and teardown reasons, strong TLS/DNS/DHCP semantics, independent sensor UIDs and observation differences, credible Security-log clearing, role-sensitive activity, and useful investigative pivots.
- Those strengths do not neutralize concrete semantic contradictions or fleet-wide source-native regularities. The panel distinguishes valid cross-source invariants from mere “complete matching.”
- The reports consistently exclude sanitized domains, source selection or absence, mere complete cross-source matching, and a clean or compact narrative as adverse evidence by themselves. None contributed to the revised scores.
- The highest-value remediation targets are the canonical proxy/TCP lifecycle, date- and host-coherent software inventory, and source-native endpoint enrichment/timing distributions.

## Key Disagreements

**Round 2 — cross-examination**

- **Whether the initial corpus contained a hard contradiction.** The Threat Hunter and Host/EDR reports found none, while Network Forensics identified the proxy tunnel/TCP lifetime violation. The latter is stronger because it is a necessary lifecycle invariant, occurs 416 times, and was independently reproduced from the frozen data; 262 exact byte matches sharply reduce alternate-pairing doubt.
- **How decisive software chronology is.** Detection Engineering treats later release trains on 18 March 2024 as critical. The frozen records verify the timestamps and exact versions—Teams `24124.2315.3009.6699`, VS Code `1.89.1`, and Office `16.0.17628.20006`—but the allowed materials contain no release catalog. The panel therefore preserves the Detection Engineer's specialist conclusion while separating it from claims independently provable using only the corpus.
- **How to weight Linux regularity.** The Threat Hunter viewed synchronized `debian-sa1` behavior and smooth PID velocity as moderate generator-like texture; Host/EDR considered differing cron phases and PID bases credible fleet variation. The data confirms the command on ten Linux hosts, but central image management remains a plausible alternative, so this stays supporting rather than decisive evidence.
- **How suspicious the Type 9/proxy identity mismatch is.** The Threat Hunter identified `marcus.chen` as the Type 9 outbound principal while the matching upload is attributed to `aisha.johnson`; the records confirm both facts. Cached SSO, IP-based attribution, or separate proxy authentication could explain the difference, so the panel retains it as a contract gap rather than a contradiction.
- **How much weight endpoint micro-repetition deserves.** The Threat Hunter treated millisecond-separated identical eCAR access records as low-to-moderate evidence; Host/EDR assigned greater weight after combining 103 arithmetic ProcessAccess clusters with the dataset-wide three-frame call-stack ceiling. The broader source-native pattern is persuasive, but real tight API loops remain a valid alternative explanation for individual repeats.

## Most Convincing Evidence

1. **Proxy tunnels outlive their carrying TCP sessions.** Independent recalculation reproduced 639 uniquely keyed completed sessions, 416 duration violations, and 262 violations with exact two-way byte agreement. This is the panel's strongest evidence because it is systemic and violates a necessary application/transport lifecycle bound.
2. **Software identity is not temporally or product-generation coherent.** The corpus confirms March 2024 events carrying the cited Teams, VS Code, and Office versions; Teams also repeatedly combines version `24124.2315.3009.6699` with `AppData\Local\Microsoft\Teams\current\Teams.exe` on four hosts. The release chronology and generation interpretation come from the Detection Engineer's specialty.
3. **Sysmon ProcessAccess has a fleet-wide stack-depth ceiling.** Recalculation confirms 713 Event 10 records: 149 one-frame, 20 two-frame, and 544 three-frame call traces, with none longer. Its breadth across hosts and behaviors makes ordinary per-process variation an inadequate explanation.
4. **Near-exact 600 ms proxy pacing recurs across unrelated clients.** Recalculation confirms 1,487 client-to-proxy connections, 131 adjacent gaps in the 0.595–0.605-second band, and 30 runs of three to six connections across eight clients. Cross-client reuse of the cadence is stronger than a single burst that could be normal browser behavior.
5. **Endpoint enrichment fails in catalog-shaped clusters.** Recalculation confirms 19 of 972 process starts with `Hashes=-` and 100 with all five PE metadata fields absent, including repeated executable families and storyline-relevant tools. Real acquisition failures are possible, but concentration by executable identity supports the Host/EDR inference of finite-catalog behavior.

## Most Debated Points

- **Could proxy mismatch or sensor timing explain the tunnel-duration result?** Alternate flow matching, clock offset, or collection loss were challenged. Unique source-port matching, completed `SF` state, hundreds of violations, and exact byte equality in 262 cases leave no credible explanation for an inner CONNECT tunnel lasting longer than its outer TCP connection.
- **Could the software dates be intentionally shifted or sanitized?** A timestamp shift could reconcile later software, but no allowed record exposes such a policy. Because sanitized domains are expressly excluded and no timestamp-sanitization claim appears in the corpus, the panel does not invent that explanation; it does, however, label release chronology as specialist knowledge rather than corpus-only proof.
- **Could centralized administration explain Linux cron and PID patterns?** It plausibly explains shared `debian-sa1` commands and fixed schedules, especially in a managed fleet. It explains smooth, similar PID velocity less completely, but these observations remain distribution texture and are not needed for the final verdict.
- **Could proxy authentication be independent of Type 9 credentials?** Yes. Cached browser/SSO state, source-IP attribution, or separate credentials could produce `aisha.johnson` at the proxy while the local Type 9 token carries `marcus.chen`; absent a visible transition, this remains ambiguous.
- **Are short call traces and missing metadata normal filtering or collection effects?** Individual instances can be. The debate turns on fleet-wide shape: no Event 10 stack exceeds three frames, and enrichment gaps cluster by executable rather than appearing as a diffuse acquisition-loss population.
- **Do the dataset's many strengths argue against synthesis?** They lower confidence when evidence is merely neat or complete, but they cannot negate a repeated lifecycle impossibility. The panel therefore preserves these strengths as successful realism features while still reaching a synthetic verdict.

## Improvement Recommendations (Consensus)

1. Make the client-to-proxy TCP connection the authoritative outer lifecycle for every CONNECT transaction. Derive connection close, tunnel close, duration, byte accounting, and proxy records from one lifecycle, and enforce `CONNECT setup + tunnel duration <= TCP duration` using client source port and tunnel identity.
2. Replace the 600 ms scheduler signature with session-level browser behavior: parallel resource discovery, bounded connection pools, persistent reuse, variable request dependencies, and nonuniform client- and destination-specific delays. Test distributions across clients with gap histograms and run-length checks.
3. Build a date-valid, per-host software inventory before rendering process metadata. Validate application availability against event time, model classic and new Teams as distinct products, and keep core Windows binaries within the host's servicing lineage unless explicit copied/side-loaded file evidence explains an exception.
4. Make Sysmon enrichment source-native rather than catalog-complete or catalog-empty. Generate realistic ProcessAccess stack-depth/module distributions; populate configured hashes for accessible images; and model hash or PE-metadata failure with explicit, independently varying acquisition conditions.
5. Give proxy authentication and ACL decisions durable state. Successful retries after 407 responses should expose the authenticated principal when the format supports it, while 403/407/200 outcomes should follow observable policy inputs rather than per-request sampling.
6. Diversify fleet timing without discarding managed-environment regularity. Preserve some centrally scheduled jobs, but vary cron/systemd mechanisms, host-role cadence, missed runs, restart effects, PID pressure, authentication reaction times, and SSH/RDP stage delays.
7. Repair lower-weight canonical contracts: keep same-sensor HTTP FUID references atomic with file observations, make database archive creation precede hash/size checks unless replacement history is visible, and clarify Type 9 versus proxy identity ownership when separate authentication mechanisms are intended.
8. Preserve the production-like features all experts recognized: lifecycle ordering, native schema detail, firewall/Zeek state semantics, TLS/DNS/DHCP fidelity, sensor-local UIDs and capture variation, role-sensitive traffic, Security-log reset behavior, and Postfix queue chains.
