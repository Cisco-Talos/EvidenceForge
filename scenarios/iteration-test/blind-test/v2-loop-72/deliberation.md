# Deliberation Summary

## Panel Composition

| Expert | Initial Verdict | Initial Verdict Confidence | Initial Synthetic-Confidence | Final Verdict | Final Verdict Confidence | Final Synthetic-Confidence |
|--------|----------------|----------------------------|------------------------------|---------------|--------------------------|----------------------------|
| Threat Hunter | Synthetic | 70 | 66 | Synthetic | 80 | 72 |

Round 1 position summaries:

- **Threat Hunter:** Initially judged the data Synthetic because two unrelated shell histories inspect exact artifacts before their observed creation, SMB paths and filenames show patterned vocabulary, novel service binaries lack visible delivery, and one reported SSH receiver lifecycle is close-only in syslog. The strongest specialty-specific counterevidence was realistic role-shaped volume, operationally useful pivots, coherent action lifecycles, and credible multi-leg transfer accounting.
- **Detection Engineer:** Initially judged the data Synthetic, led by 120 of 214 tightly matched Kerberos Type-3 logons occurring before nearby same-client/account AS requests, including an LDAP flow and successful logon before the visible UDP/88 exchange. Unique supporting evidence included only eight Sysmon Event 10 module-sequence shapes across 713 records, literal `history:"-"` in all 712 ICMP connections, and inconsistent missing-IP representation in eCAR sessions.
- **Network Forensics:** Initially judged the data Real because tuple ownership, Zeek parent/child timing, DNS cache TTL decay, state/history diversity, independent sensor UIDs and clocks, certificate graphs, packet accounting, and role-aligned traffic were production-like. Its unique reservation was a clean SSL publication boundary: all 2,496 emitted SSL rows are established and have `SF` parents, while 207 connections already classified as SSL have no SSL child.
- **Host/EDR Forensics:** Initially judged the data Real because 971 matched Windows process starts agree across eCAR, Sysmon, and Security, with no visible dependent-before-create or post-termination use; Windows, RDP, SSH/PAM, sudo, and audit-log-clear behavior was source-native and coherent. Unique concerns were five Type-7 unlocks that create new eCAR session object IDs for existing logon/session identities, overlapping same-user local sessions on four workstations, sparse role-convergent process volume, and a tightly bounded Security-after-Sysmon timing envelope.

After cross-examination, the Threat Hunter raised synthetic confidence because the repeated Kerberos inversion was independently reproduced, while reducing the weight assigned to future-year SMB paths after its numerator was corrected. The Detection Engineer retained and slightly strengthened the Synthetic verdict because the principal Kerberos, Event 10, gzip, and ICMP measurements reproduced exactly; the strong Host and Network findings constrained the score from rising further. Network Forensics moved to Inconclusive because the verified LDAP/logon-before-Kerberos example crosses its transport specialty, but retained substantial weight on the otherwise production-like network corpus and on ticket-cache ambiguity. Host/EDR Forensics retained Real with lower verdict confidence and higher synthetic confidence: the DC authentication timing is material, but the bulk process/session evidence remains contradiction-free and same-account/IP proximity alone does not prove that every later 4768 funded the earlier logon.

## Key Agreements

- The corpus is technically strong at the field and relationship level. All reviewers reported parseable source formats, stable identities, credible host roles, and useful cross-source pivots.
- Process, session, tuple, Zeek UID/FUID, TLS certificate, and connection-lifetime checks are broadly coherent. No reviewer found a dataset-wide terminate-before-create, child-before-parent, invalid tuple ownership, or protocol-outside-connection failure.
- The disputed negative findings are log-visible defects or repeatable distribution patterns, not penalties for a clean narrative, broad coverage, or easy huntability.
- Independent review reproduced the central measurements: 120 of 214 matched Kerberos logons precede the nearby 4768; Sysmon Event 10 has 713 records, eight stack shapes, and no trace deeper than three frames; all 2,496 SSL rows are established and have `SF` parents while 207 SSL-classified parents lack children; all 712 ICMP connections use `history:"-"`; the gzip output is inspected before observed creation; and five Type-7 unlocks split the eCAR session object identity.
- The panel agreed that the existing strengths—role-shaped traffic, independent sensor/host timing, bounded-window lifecycle handling, stable process identity, DNS cache behavior, certificate linkage, and multi-leg transfer accounting—should be preserved during remediation.

## Key Disagreements

- **Kerberos severity:** Detection treats the repeated ordering as a hard contradiction and the decisive authenticity signal. Threat Hunting accepts it as strong systemic evidence. Network and Host give it substantial weight but stop short of treating all 120 matches as individually proven dependencies because a successful logon can consume a cached ticket and a nearby same-account/IP 4768 can be a separate exchange. The exact `WS-DRAMIREZ-01$` case is harder to dismiss: LDAP transport and Event 4624 precede the visible port-88 transport and Events 4768/4769.
- **Whether strong coherence favors Real:** Network and Host regard varied sensor observations, exact source-native semantics, and contradiction-free process/session lifecycles as affirmative production evidence. Threat Hunting and Detection agree those properties are realistic but consider the repeated causal and distribution defects more diagnostic.
- **Collection-policy explanations:** Network considers missing SSL/file/mapping children plausibly attributable to filtering or loss; Threat Hunting similarly allows pre-window service binaries and selective syslog loss. Detection argues the perfectly clean SSL-success boundary and other systematic sentinels look implemented rather than incidentally lost.
- **Distribution evidence:** Threat Hunting and Detection assign material weight to SMB vocabulary/year patterns and shallow Event 10 stacks. Network and Host accept these as concerns but give more weight to DNS, transport, process, and timing diversity elsewhere in the corpus.
- The panel did not reach a verdict consensus: two experts remain Synthetic, one is Inconclusive, and one remains Real with reduced confidence.

## Most Convincing Evidence

1. **Repeated Kerberos dependency inversion — synthetic indicator.** Independent parsing reproduced 214 same-account/client matches and the 120/94 reversed-versus-expected split across both DCs. The concrete DC-01 example places LDAP at epoch `1710764069.702474`, Event 4624 at `12:14:29.9307193Z`, UDP/88 at `1710764070.610033`, and Events 4768/4769 afterward. The panel judged the repeated narrow bidirectional timing pattern more persuasive than any individual proximity match, while retaining the cached-ticket caveat.
2. **Artifact-before-creation sequence — synthetic indicator.** DB-PROD root hashes `/tmp/rpt_0318.sql.gz` at epoch `1710782100` and stats it at `1710782111`, then runs gzip at `1710782112`; eCAR places gzip creation at `1710782112935` ms and the file create at `1710782116883` ms. Shell commands could fail, but an actually pre-existing gzip file would also make ordinary gzip without force refuse replacement, so the later successful-looking transfer makes the alternative explanation strained.
3. **Shallow Sysmon Event 10 grammar — synthetic indicator.** The reported 713 records, frame-count split of 149 one-frame, 20 two-frame, and 544 three-frame traces, and eight total module-sequence shapes reproduced exactly. The hard three-frame ceiling across heterogeneous process pairs is a strong distributional tell even though individual call traces are source-shaped.
4. **Success-only SSL publication boundary — synthetic indicator with a collection-policy alternative.** Across all sensors, every emitted SSL row is `established:true` and every parent is `SF`; 207 recognized SSL parents lack a child, including 195 non-`SF` and 12 `SF` connections. An explicit analyzer/export policy could explain this, but no visible policy explains the deterministic boundary.
5. **Cross-source lifecycle and observation coherence — real indicator.** The panel consistently credited stable process/session identities, valid Zeek parent/child timing, independently jittered sensor and endpoint observations, DNS TTL countdowns, TLS/X.509 linkage, source-native audit-log clearing, and credible role-based traffic. These checks prevent the isolated defects from supporting a confidently synthetic verdict.

## Most Debated Points

- Whether the 120 Kerberos matches establish 120 direct causal inversions or a strong aggregate timing fingerprint containing some cached-ticket or unrelated-renewal matches. The aggregate split and the exact LDAP-before-port-88 example survived scrutiny; the universal “hard contradiction” label did not achieve full-panel agreement.
- Whether the early `sha256sum` and `du` commands prove impossible file use. The visible order is confirmed, but shell history has no exit status; the panel therefore treated it as highly suspicious rather than unqualified proof.
- Whether the SSL gap reflects generator admission logic or a real collection/export boundary. The all-`SF`, all-established publication shape is systematic, while the surrounding packet-loss and multi-sensor behavior is otherwise convincingly imperfect.
- The Threat Hunter's future-year SMB numerator was corrected during verification. There are 18 future-year names among 112 `SMB::FILE_OPEN` rows; 33 is the count across all SMB operation rows, including reads and writes. Future-dated SYSVOL/NETLOGON names remain questionable, but the corrected proportion lowers this finding's weight.
- The five eCAR Type-7 unlock identity splits are real and repeated, but the Host reviewer allowed that they could reflect a product-specific normalization convention rather than fabricated raw telemetry.
- The Threat Hunter's receiver-side close-only SSH syslog claim could not be independently rechecked because the frozen review-data directory available to the deliberation contains no syslog file. It remains low-weight report evidence rather than a verified panel finding.

## Improvement Recommendations (Consensus)

The verdicts remain split, but the panel agreed that these evidence-bound changes would make the corpus harder to distinguish from production telemetry:

1. **Protect Kerberos causal edges as one ordered activity.** Ensure client port-88 transport and Events 4768/4769 precede the successful Kerberos 4624 and its service transport when a newly issued ticket is being modeled. Apply source jitter without crossing those boundaries, and validate the relationship by account, client IP, service, and bounded time.
2. **Make artifact state explicit before commands consume it.** Create and compress files before successful `file`, `du`, hash, read, or transfer activity. If a preflight probe is intentionally issued against an absent or stale file, emit an observable failure or explicit pre-existing-file state, and make overwrite flags and later file identity consistent.
3. **Model TLS analyzer visibility independently of clean connection closure.** Emit plausible `established:false` or partial-handshake SSL rows for selected recognized reset/partial connections. If filtering or sampling is intentional, apply and expose a coherent source-local policy instead of an unexplained all-`SF` publication rule; retain referenced HTTP files and SMB mappings under the same policy.
4. **Broaden source-native distribution texture.** Vary Sysmon Event 10 stack depth and module sequences by source/target process, access mask, OS/build, and loaded modules. Anchor operational SYSVOL/NETLOGON years to the collection date, reserve future years for clearly forward-looking content, and expand SMB filename vocabulary beyond repeated `draft`, `review`, `final`, `approved`, and `v2` combinations.
5. **Normalize durable identities and missing values consistently.** Keep one eCAR USER_SESSION object identity across Type-2 login, Type-7 unlock, and logout for the same logon/session, unless the normalization contract explicitly requires a new object. Omit or null unavailable eCAR IP values and unavailable Zeek JSON history rather than serializing text-log `"-"` sentinels.
6. **Preserve verified realism while fixing defects.** Retain independent sensor UIDs and clock texture, DNS cache-state behavior, directional loss/history coupling, exact process and session correlations, active-at-cutoff lifecycles, certificate graphs, responder-side flow semantics, and multi-leg byte/tuple accounting.
