The Threat Hunter retained a Synthetic verdict and increased both confidence measures slightly after the Network Forensics findings established that the HTTP problem was a repeated proxy-contract family, not only one inconsistent field. The Detection Engineer moved from Real to Inconclusive because the cross-source Referer mismatch and six proxy authority mutations directly challenge the initial statement that no protocol-field contradiction existed; the strong source-native contracts kept the revised synthetic-confidence below 50. Network Forensics also moved from Real to Inconclusive after adding the independently found Referer conflict and host-wide distribution findings to the already observed authority mutations. Host/EDR Forensics retained a Synthetic verdict but reduced verdict confidence and synthetic-confidence because verification supported the exact eight-record D-Bus quota on ten hosts but not the claimed exact four-record resolver quota on ten hosts: the observed resolver counts were mixed, including four, five, and zero.

The final mean synthetic-confidence is 57/100. The panel therefore reaches an overall **Inconclusive, synthetic-leaning** assessment rather than unanimity: two experts retain Synthetic verdicts and two now judge the evidence genuinely balanced.

## Key Agreements

Round 1 established the following independent positions and strongest specialty evidence:

- **Threat Hunter:** Synthetic at 78% verdict confidence and 66 synthetic-confidence. The strongest evidence was the exact support-upload Referer disagreement between both Zeek views and the matching proxy record; two archive-inspection sequences that precede recorded archive creation; and the repeated 2025–2027, suffix-heavy SMB paths, including GPO-like locations under NETLOGON and SYSVOL. The strongest counterevidence was coherent endpoint/session lifecycles and pivotable credential theft, remote administration, persistence, and exfiltration evidence.
- **Detection Engineer:** Real at 72% verdict confidence and 34 synthetic-confidence. The strongest synthetic indicators were the one-to-three-frame ceiling across 713 Sysmon Event 10 `CallTrace` values, selective `Hashes=-` on 19 of 972 Sysmon process creates, and repeated Linux daemon prose. The strongest real indicators were accurate Windows event contracts, correct Security `EventRecordID` reset after event 1102, coherent Zeek file/loss accounting, and source-native timing differences.
- **Network Forensics:** Real at 78% verdict confidence and 36 synthetic-confidence. The unique high-weight finding was six cleartext proxy pairs in which client authority changed to an unrelated IP-literal outbound `Host`; weaker findings were TLD-linked generated-looking DNS vocabulary and limited ordinary HTTP reuse. Counterevidence included coherent transport arithmetic and states, independent sensor-clock drift, sensor-specific loss, valid DNS/TLS behavior, and sessions left open at the collection cutoff.
- **Host/EDR Forensics:** Synthetic at 80% verdict confidence and 64 synthetic-confidence. The strongest evidence was fleet-wide Linux category repetition, co-launched competing endpoint access products, overlapping client instances, and an integration worker repeatedly running on a domain controller. Counterevidence included strong process/session lifecycles, cross-source PID and principal reconciliation, the DC-01 Security-log-clear sequence, and coherent SSH/SCP lineage.

All four experts agree that the corpus has high source-native format fidelity, strong identifier and lifecycle semantics, useful cross-source pivots, varied transport outcomes, and credible source-specific timestamp behavior. They also agree that the remaining adverse evidence is concentrated in a few contract and distribution families rather than broad schema failure. None treats a clean attack narrative, suspicious activity by itself, complete cross-source matching, or generally rich coverage as evidence of synthesis.

## Key Disagreements

The main Round 2 contradiction was between the Detection Engineer's finding of no protocol-field conflict and the Threat Hunter's exact Referer mismatch. Verification favored the Threat Hunter's evidence: both Zeek sensors record `https://www.reddit.com/` for the same 16:28:07 support POST, while the matching proxy row records `"-"`. A proxy-specific omission or sanitized export remains possible, but it is weakened by the reported 1,570 agreeing Referers among 1,571 comparable Zeek/proxy pairs. Without packet content or a documented proxy transformation policy, the panel does not elevate the single mismatch to proof of synthesis.

The Network Forensics verdict initially treated six outbound `Host` mutations as outweighed by realistic network behavior. Cross-examination established that these are repeated semantic inconsistencies: the client-visible authority is replaced on the proxy-to-origin leg by an IP literal that is sometimes unrelated to both the authority and connected destination. Sanitization could explain the class only if correlated authority fields were transformed inconsistently; that is an alternative cause, not a defense of the resulting telemetry's realism.

The Synthetic reviewers gave more weight to fleet and environment texture than the Real reviewers. The exact eight-record D-Bus count across ten Linux hosts is concrete and remains difficult to explain from sharply different host volumes, but the Host/EDR report's companion claim of exactly four resolver records on ten hosts was not borne out by verification. Co-installed access agents, overlapping UI processes, a Java integration worker on a domain controller, verbose `irqbalance`/`snapd`, rare-TLD naming, and future-year SMB paths remain plausible in migrations, labs, unusual enterprise deployments, or sanitized collections. The disagreement is therefore about cumulative probability, not impossibility.

The archive sequences remain disputed. On DB-PROD-01, `sha256sum` and `du` target `/tmp/rpt_0318.sql.gz` before the visible `gzip` command and endpoint file creation; on WS-LNGUYEN-01, `file` and `du` target an archive well before the visible `tar` creation. These are operationally backward if success was intended, but shell history does not prove success and a stale pre-existing file could explain either case. Their recurrence raises suspicion without establishing impossible ordering.

Claims based only on absent source families, a missing pre-window initiator, a single missing companion record, complete UID/reference integrity, broad telemetry completeness, or an unusually clean and narratable intrusion were rejected as invalid discriminators. The isolated SMB mapping omission was treated as plausible collection loss, and the clean attack progression was excluded from both verdict revisions and recommendations.

## Most Convincing Evidence

1. **Repeated proxy authority contract defects — synthetic-leaning, strongest.** Six cleartext proxy transactions mutate the client authority into an IP-literal outbound `Host`, including values unrelated to the connected destination. Repetition across a transaction family makes this stronger than an isolated formatting anomaly.
2. **Exact cross-source Referer disagreement — synthetic-leaning, strong.** Both Zeek views agree that one support POST carried a Reddit Referer, while the matching proxy record omits it; almost all comparable requests reportedly agree. The narrowness of the anomaly and lack of packet evidence prevent it from being conclusive.
3. **Independent observation texture — real-leaning, strong.** Sensor-specific clock offsets drift over time, capture loss differs by sensor and flow, TCP outcomes and durations vary, and late connections remain active at cutoff. These are substantive collection behaviors, not mere completeness.
4. **Bounded fleet distributions — synthetic-leaning, moderate to strong.** Ten Linux hosts have exactly eight D-Bus records despite different roles and total volumes, and all 713 examined Sysmon Event 10 traces stop at three frames while reusing a small pattern vocabulary. The resolver-quota overstatement reduces, but does not remove, this family-level concern.
5. **Source-native host contracts — real-leaning, strong.** Process and session lifecycles remain ordered across eCAR, Sysmon, Security, syslog, and bash history; the DC-01 event 1102 sequence correctly resets Security record numbering; and SSH/SCP sessions preserve tuple, principal, process, file, and closure relationships.

## Most Debated Points

- **Whether the HTTP defects prove synthesis.** The panel agrees they are real defects in the reviewed telemetry. It disagrees on whether inconsistent sanitization, proxy field handling, or export transformation is plausible enough to keep them from determining authenticity.
- **Whether cumulative texture outweighs deep realism.** The Synthetic reviewers regard fixed D-Bus counts, shallow call traces, repeated service prose, future-year GPO-like paths, and software-role mixing as mutually reinforcing catalog fingerprints. The other reviewers view each as individually explainable and give more weight to stateful source-native details that survive broad checks.
- **Whether archive ordering is a contract gap or ordinary operator behavior.** A failed command, stale file, or abandoned workflow is possible. No exit-status or file-metadata evidence resolves the question, so the finding remains moderate rather than hard.
- **How much to penalize environment oddities.** Multiple VPN/SSE products and business middleware on a domain controller are atypical, but real environments often contain migrations, exceptions, and technical debt. The panel treats repeated deployment across hosts as suspicious texture, not contradiction.
- **What the excellent correlations mean.** They establish that the data is coherent and useful, but do not establish authenticity by themselves. The panel explicitly avoids converting completeness, clean pivots, or lack of missing records into either a real or synthetic verdict.

## Improvement Recommendations (Consensus)

1. Preserve one canonical HTTP authority and header set across client, proxy, DNS, destination selection, outbound `Host`, TLS SNI, and logged proxy fields. Add family-wide checks for both the six authority mutations and the isolated Referer disagreement, while allowing explicitly modeled proxy transformations.
2. Replace fixed Linux category budgets with host-state- and demand-driven activity. Permit zero events, broad count variance, role-specific service enablement, and activity throughout the window; vary `irqbalance`, `snapd`, D-Bus, resolver, and anacron vocabulary and cadence by host configuration.
3. Expand Sysmon Event 10 traces with access-path-, binary-, and OS-conditioned depth and modules. Model missing hashes from explicit collection mechanics such as access failures, exclusions, races, or timeouts rather than selecting binaries by narrative importance.
4. Enforce organization-level deployment and host-role constraints. Select a primary endpoint access stack per workstation unless an explicit migration cohort is modeled, prevent overlapping singleton UI instances without restart/upgrade evidence, and require an explicit exception before placing business middleware on a domain controller.
5. Validate file-workflow dependencies before emitting commands that inspect or transfer a named output. If a pre-creation inspection is intentional, make the failed result or pre-existing-file state visible enough that the sequence is not indistinguishable from planner inversion.
6. Diversify environmental naming pools and tie them to stable entities. Reduce formulaic future-year and `-v2`/`-final`/`-review` SMB paths—especially under SYSVOL/NETLOGON—and TLD-specific DNS prefix vocabularies unless organizational or software context supports them.
7. Preserve the strongest realism features while applying these changes: source-specific clock drift and latency, asymmetric capture loss, varied TCP termination, active-at-cutoff lifecycles, Security record-number reset, source-native identifiers, and cross-source process/session/transport/file causality.
