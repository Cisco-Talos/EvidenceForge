# Network Forensics Analyst — Authenticity Assessment

## Verdict

**Assessment:** Synthetic  
**Verdict Confidence:** 78  
**Synthetic-Confidence Score:** 68

## Executive Summary

The network evidence is technically strong at the individual-flow and cross-sensor levels, but a dataset-wide clock-hour boundary trough and source-inconsistent proxy response texture are difficult to reconcile with organically occurring production traffic. I assess the data as synthetic with moderately high confidence, while keeping the synthetic-confidence score below the top band because I found no impossible tuple, protocol, DNS, certificate, or visible causal ordering contradiction.

## Evidence For Synthetic

- `[distribution_texture]` **Repeated clock-hour boundary trough across independent source families.** The visible window is 2024-03-18 12:00:03–17:59:53 UTC. After excluding the short 14:31 scan burst, `zeek-core/conn.json` contains only 39, 52, 41, 46, 118, and 40 records in the six successive `:55–:59` five-minute bins. The matching `:00–:04` bins contain 44, 55, 40, 71, 60, and 66 records, while the ten interior five-minute bins average roughly 134–181 records per bin in each hour. Aggregated by minute-of-hour, counts fall from 156–213 during `:50–:54` to 112, 52, 47, 63, and 62 during minutes 55–59, then remain low at minutes 0–4 (45, 64, 45, 67, 115) before jumping to 167 at minute 5. The same boundary depression is visible in core DNS (slot 11: 170 vs. 206–359 in most interior slots), core HTTP (32 vs. 106–191), core TLS (9 vs. 22–40), DMZ connections (169 vs. 514–877), and all eCAR records (1,520 vs. 2,572–4,272). A synchronized ten-minute occurrence-time trough centered on every hour boundary across Zeek protocol logs, multiple sensors, and endpoint telemetry is a strong generator-window fingerprint, not just a source-volume issue.
- `[distribution_texture]` **One proxy endpoint emits unusually randomized reason phrases and body sizes for the same status paths.** In `zeek-core/http.json`, all relevant responses come from `10.10.3.20:8080`, yet 403 alternates between `Access Denied` (17 records) and `Forbidden` (30), 407 alternates between `Authentication Required` (11) and `Proxy Authentication Required` (27), 502 alternates between `Bad Gateway` and `Upstream Connection Failed`, and 504 rotates among `Gateway Timeout`, `Connection Timed Out`, and `Upstream Timeout`. Concrete examples include `pypi.org` at 1710763993.303869 with `407 Authentication Required`, `ctldl.windowsupdate.com` at 1710764031.910065 with `407 Proxy Authentication Required`, `api.mixpanel.com` at 1710763682.238381 with `403 Forbidden`, and `calendar.google.com` at 1710768275.546660 with `403 Access Denied`. Within the core sensor, response-body length is also nearly unique per error occurrence: 17/17 unique for `403 Access Denied`, 29/30 for `403 Forbidden`, 11/11 for `407 Authentication Required`, and 27/27 for `407 Proxy Authentication Required`. A real appliance or stable proxy tier normally has a much smaller set of response templates and reason phrases tied to identifiable policy or failure paths; this looks like independent selection from phrase and size pools.
- `[weak_signal]` **Successful TLS observations have a constrained long tail.** Across 2,429 `ssl.json` rows, every record has `established:true`; TLS 1.3 uses only `CSD`, `CSDD`, or `CSDDD`, and TLS 1.2 uses only five history strings. This is not a contradiction—failed TCP and proxy attempts are represented elsewhere—but the complete absence of any logged non-established TLS negotiation across a varied six-hour enterprise/internet mix reinforces the smoother-than-production texture. I assign this only low weight because Zeek logging policy can suppress incomplete handshakes.

## Evidence For Real

- **Believable connection-state and service diversity.** The 19,217 Zeek connection rows contain 14,101 `SF`, 4,557 `S0`, 238 `RSTO`, 157 `RSTR`, 49 `REJ`, and smaller `S1`/`S2`/`S3`/`OTH` populations. TCP histories are compatible with the states, byte-bearing directions always have packets, IP-byte counts exceed payload counts by enough to carry headers, and no same-sensor five-tuple intervals overlap.
- **The large scan behaves like a real SYN scan rather than generic noise.** Around 1710769896–1710769907, `10.10.3.10` probes 254 targets on ports 22, 80, 443, 445, and 3306 without duplicate target/port pairs. Most attempts are `S0`, while live hosts produce source-native distinctions: `10.10.2.20:445` is `SF`/`smb` at 1710769897.351839, `10.10.2.26:443` is `REJ` at 1710769897.014989, and `10.10.2.30:22` is `SF`/`ssh` at 1710769900.179882.
- **Independent sensors retain sensor-local character.** I matched 4,055 physical five-tuples between core and DMZ. Their Zeek UIDs differ, their clock offset varies narrowly from 0.110322 to 0.118541 seconds, and 356 histories plus roughly 200 packet/byte counters differ even though connection state never disagrees. The 127 core/database matches show a different offset range (-0.074635 to -0.054387 seconds) and their own packet/history differences. That is much more credible than cloning one canonical row unchanged into every sensor.
- **DNS is protocol-coherent and environmentally varied.** The 3,887 DNS rows include A (2,885), AAAA (363), TXT (316), PTR (191), SRV (107), MX, SOA, and NS queries; responses include 361 NXDOMAINs, 26 SERVFAILs, and three REFUSED results. Windows-like `wpad`, `isatap`, stale-printer, short-name/suffix-search, PTR, AD LDAP/Kerberos SRV, and DNS-tunnel-like TXT traffic coexist. Every DNS row matches a same-sensor `conn.json` UID and tuple, DNS timestamps follow connection open by about 1–71 ms, and every recorded RTT fits inside its connection duration.
- **TLS certificate semantics are unusually convincing.** Version/cipher combinations are valid, all 548 checked SNI-bearing handshakes with a visible leaf certificate match the subject/SAN, every listed certificate FUID exists in the corresponding `x509.json`, every two-element chain has leaf issuer equal to intermediate subject, and no certificate is used outside its validity interval. The 37 non-resumed TLS 1.2 rows lacking `cert_chain_fuids` initially look suspicious, but every one has nonzero `missed_bytes` in its associated connection (for example UID `CaalbmLvZNWUgdOx48`, SNI `FILE-LNX-01.meridianhcs.local`, 18 missed bytes at 1710763760.975925), providing a concrete packet-loss explanation rather than an orphaned certificate contract.
- **HTTP byte accounting respects capture loss.** For every HTTP UID, summed request and response body sizes fit inside connection-direction bytes plus `missed_bytes`. UID `CSBMReILR36ahgb6Kg` is a useful edge case: its three response bodies total 526,578 bytes, exceeding `resp_bytes` 521,723, but the connection records 5,476 missed bytes, making the accounting feasible.
- **Proxy terminal behavior is causally sensible.** Of 1,292 successful CONNECT transactions in the core sensor, 1,285 have a matching proxy-origin TLS SNI within ten seconds. The 103 failed CONNECT transactions do not trigger corresponding immediate egress TLS, aside from one nearest same-host event 6.25 seconds earlier that is not causally downstream. This supports correct separation between client-to-proxy success and proxy-to-origin terminal outcomes.
- **Endpoint FLOW direction agrees with host identity.** Modal host/IP mappings derived solely from eCAR FLOW rows are stable for all observed systems (for example `PROXY-01`→`10.10.3.20`, `WEB-EXT-01`→`10.10.3.10`, `DB-PROD-01`→`10.10.4.10`); every OUTBOUND row uses the host IP as source and every INBOUND row uses it as destination. Of 23,652 eCAR FLOW rows, 22,905 match a Zeek tuple within five seconds; most unmatched records are ICMP representations without ports or local/self DNS flows that a network sensor would not necessarily observe.

## Detailed Analysis

**Scope and source inventory.** I examined only the supplied data directory. The network-facing material comprises three Zeek observation points: core (10,878 connections), DMZ (7,919), and database (420), spanning almost exactly six hours on Monday, 2024-03-18. Protocol logs include 3,887 DNS, 3,408 HTTP, 2,429 TLS/SSL, 1,041 X.509, 94 OCSP, 2,088 files, 48 DHCP, 361 SMB-file, 213 SMB-mapping, and 46 SMTP records. I found no separate Suricata/IDS, firewall, or dedicated proxy-access file in the allowed directory; under the study rules, I did not treat that absence as evidence in either direction. Proxy semantics are observable through HTTP CONNECT and the two network legs, while eCAR supplies endpoint FLOW observations.

**Connection and flow semantics.** TCP, UDP, and ICMP are represented with plausible port and state distributions. Port 53 leads with 3,915 Zeek connections, followed by 443 (3,247), explicit proxy 8080 (3,141), Kerberos 88 (2,202), LDAP 389 (1,204), MySQL 3306 (1,050), SMB 445 (1,013), and HTTP 80 (916), with smaller SSH, syslog, SMTP, RDP, WinRM, IMAPS, LDAPS, and other populations. Completed TCP duration has a realistic broad shape (median 2.319 seconds, 90th percentile 6.398 seconds, 99th percentile 80.552 seconds, maximum 15,424.919 seconds). The state/history pairings and scan responses are internally valid, and the eCAR success/failure view generally agrees: explicit `failure` maps to S0, REJ, reset, partial, or odd-state Zeek outcomes, while unmarked TCP FLOW rows overwhelmingly map to `SF`. Unmarked eCAR flows mapping to `S0` are almost entirely one-way UDP syslog, where lack of a response is not application failure.

The multi-sensor treatment is a major strength. Core/DMZ copies of one physical tuple use different UIDs and slightly different timestamps and counters, as real independent Zeek instances do. For example, the client-proxy tuple `10.10.2.25:53891`→`10.10.3.20:8080` appears in DMZ as UID `CUsQECDzjGwSoS5avwK` at 1710763440.504138 and in core as `Cc4Z6vt7uUzTHHReI09` at 1710763440.617781; durations differ by approximately one millisecond rather than being bit-identical.

**DNS.** Query/response structure is sound: class/type numeric and text fields agree, successful internal responses are authoritative, recursive external responses are non-authoritative, answer and TTL vector lengths are compatible, and failure responses omit answers. AD SRV answers carry plausible priority/weight/port syntax, such as `_kerberos._tcp.dc._msdcs.meridianhcs.local` returning both DCs on port 88 at 1710763230.372015. The resolver population (`10.10.2.10` and `.11`), local suffix-search failures, PTR requests, NOERROR/NODATA AAAA replies, and varied TTLs/RRTs give the source useful depth. The suspicious TXT stream from `10.10.2.30` to `*.ns1.westbridge-services.cloud` includes NOERROR, NXDOMAIN, SERVFAIL, and REFUSED outcomes rather than a single mechanically successful response path; suspiciousness itself was not used as an authenticity clue.

**HTTP and explicit-proxy behavior.** HTTP contains 2,840 CONNECT, 532 GET, and 36 POST records. CONNECT success has zero request and response body bytes, while proxy errors carry bodies; non-CONNECT keep-alives use increasing `trans_depth` values up to seven, and all HTTP timestamps/tuples remain within their associated Zeek connections. Successful CONNECTs generally produce an origin-side TLS flow from `10.10.3.20`; failed CONNECTs correctly stop at the proxy. Cross-sensor clock offsets explain small apparent inversions: after accounting for the approximately 114 ms core-vs-DMZ offset, origin setup generally follows client request reception. The authenticity concern is not the two-leg correlation but the proxy's uncontrolled response vocabulary and near-per-occurrence body sizing. The same `10.10.3.20:8080` source behaves as if it selects synonymous reason phrases independently, without an observable node, policy, client, or destination partition.

**TLS, X.509, files, and OCSP.** The TLS population is modern but mixed: 1,564 TLS 1.3 and 865 TLS 1.2 rows, with compatible AES-GCM, ChaCha20-Poly1305, CBC, RSA, and ECDSA cipher naming. Resumption is not conflated with protocol version (274 TLS 1.2 and 591 TLS 1.3 sessions are resumed), and resumed sessions omit certificate chains as expected. TLS 1.3's encrypted post-ServerHello handshake explains the broad absence of visible chains there. Visible TLS 1.2 chains link correctly into `files.json` and `x509.json`; file SHA-1 values match X.509 fingerprints, SANs cover SNI, issuer/subject chaining is consistent, and OCSP `thisUpdate`/`nextUpdate` windows contain observation time. The loss-correlated chain omissions are especially persuasive because they connect a source-native TLS artifact to packet accounting rather than simply maximizing completeness.

**Temporal texture.** At sub-event scale, timing is good: DNS occurs milliseconds after its UDP connection opens, HTTP and TLS records remain within flow intervals, DHCP renewal cadence clusters around T1 with jitter, and source-local protocol lifecycles are ordered. At aggregate scale, however, the recurring `:55–:04` valley across core, DMZ, DNS, HTTP, TLS, and eCAR is the strongest authenticity defect. It persists in every hour and across sources whose occurrence timestamps should not all pause for Zeek log rotation. The concentration is therefore not a missing-source argument; it is a repeated event-time distribution boundary.

## Realism Score by Category

- **Field format accuracy:** 9/10 — Zeek, TLS, X.509, OCSP, DNS, HTTP, SMB, and eCAR fields are structurally and semantically strong; the proxy issue is content texture rather than malformed schema.
- **Temporal patterns:** 5/10 — Event-local ordering is excellent, but the repeated cross-source hour-boundary trough is a major aggregate fingerprint.
- **Cross-source correlation:** 9/10 — Sensor-local UIDs, clock offsets, packet-loss effects, tuple matching, and proxy-leg behavior are coherent without being literal row clones.
- **Behavioral realism:** 7/10 — Scans, user/proxy traffic, infrastructure protocols, DNS failures, and lateral-service traffic are plausible, but proxy failures and TLS outcomes have limited implementation-shaped entropy.
- **Environmental consistency:** 8/10 — Host/IP direction, service placement, recursive DNS, internal PKI, DHCP, mail, proxy, DMZ, and database segmentation form a credible environment.

## Recommendations

- If this were synthetic, eliminate the per-hour generation boundary by scheduling and carrying state on a continuous timeline. Validate minute-of-hour histograms for conn, DNS, HTTP, TLS, and endpoint FLOW independently; `:55–:04` should not be systematically depleted unless an explicit, log-visible operational cause exists.
- Model proxy errors by stable appliance/node and policy-path templates. A given node should normally emit one reason phrase per status and a small family of body templates whose lengths vary only for concrete fields such as requested URL, policy name, localization, or request ID; expose a node or policy discriminator if several implementations legitimately share `10.10.3.20`.
- Add a low-rate, source-native TLS failure tail only where the collection profile would log it: negotiation alerts, client aborts, certificate failures, and incomplete handshakes should agree with `conn_state`, `ssl_history`, bytes, and packet loss. Do not add failures as independent decoration.
- Preserve the strongest existing behaviors: sensor-local Zeek UIDs, bounded clock skew, small per-sensor counter differences, packet-loss-aware certificate visibility, SNI/SAN and chain consistency, proxy terminal semantics, HTTP/connection byte accounting, and eCAR host-direction ownership.
- Do not add IDS, firewall, or proxy files merely to increase coverage. If those sources are introduced, derive them from the same visible tuple, policy, terminal outcome, and timing truth while retaining source-specific loss, delay, and schema behavior.
